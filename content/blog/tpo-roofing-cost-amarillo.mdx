TPO roofing cost for a commercial building comes down to four variables: membrane thickness, attachment method, roof size, and deck condition — not a flat per-square-foot number you can plug into a calculator. There's no single "TPO costs $X" answer that holds up across different buildings, which is why two contractors can quote the same roof thousands of dollars apart. If you're a property manager or building owner in Amarillo trying to budget for a TPO replacement or new install, understanding what actually drives the price gets you a lot further than a generic estimate.

## What Actually Changes the Price

TPO membrane comes in a few standard thicknesses — most commonly 45, 60, and 80 mil. [GAF's EverGuard TPO product line](https://www.gaf.com/en-us/roofing-materials/commercial-roofing-materials/tpo-membranes) is a good reference point: thicker membrane costs more upfront but carries a longer manufacturer warranty, running from 20 years on 45 mil membrane up to 30 years on 80 mil. Fleece-backed versions cost more than smooth membrane but resist punctures better, which matters more than people expect once hail enters the picture.

Attachment method is the second big lever. Mechanically attached systems (membrane fastened through insulation into the deck) are typically the least expensive to install. Fully adhered systems, where membrane is glued down across the whole surface, cost more in labor but perform better in high-wind conditions and hold up better over time. Ballasted systems — membrane held down with stone or pavers instead of fasteners or adhesive — are less common on commercial buildings with rooftop equipment because the ballast gets in the way of maintenance access.

Roof size matters too, but not in a straight line. A small roof with a lot of HVAC units, skylights, and roof penetrations costs more per square foot than a large, open roof, because every penetration needs flashing and detail work. Contractors price complexity, not just square footage.

Insulation is the piece building owners forget to budget for. Most commercial re-roofs require adding or upgrading rigid insulation board to meet current energy code, and the thickness required depends on your building's climate zone and existing R-value. That insulation layer sits under the membrane and gets priced separately from the TPO itself — it's a real line item, not an add-on, and it's one of the more common reasons a quote comes in higher than a building owner expected going in.

## Recover vs. Full Tear-Off

If your existing roof is in reasonably good shape — dry deck, no structural sag, no more than one existing layer — a recover (installing new TPO over the old roof) usually costs less than a full tear-off. If the deck is wet, rotted, or already has multiple layers, code and practicality both push you toward tear-off. A contractor needs to get on the roof and check the deck before quoting either option honestly; anyone who prices a recover-vs-tear-off decision from photos or a drive-by isn't giving you a real number.

## Insurance-Grade vs. Retail Pricing

Worth knowing before you start collecting quotes: insurance-scoped (Xactimate) pricing and retail cash-bid pricing aren't the same thing, even for the same job. Insurance scopes itemize every line — tear-off, underlayment, flashing, ventilation — with overhead and profit applied on qualifying claims. Retail cash bids often bundle those same items and can come in lower on paper without necessarily being a worse job. If you're comparing a quote from an insurance claim against a straight cash quote, make sure you're comparing the same scope of work, not just the bottom-line number.

## Amarillo-Specific Considerations

Potter County sits in what's sometimes called Hail Alley, where dry high-plains air meets Gulf moisture and produces frequent severe hailstorms — the Panhandle has logged 131 hail days since 2000 and typically sees 8 to 12 hailstorms a year. That history matters for TPO specifically, because thinner membrane is more vulnerable to hail bruising and puncture than thicker fleece-backed options. If your building has been hit before, or you're budgeting for the next 20 years of ownership, it's worth scheduling a [roof inspection](/roof-inspections-amarillo/) before storm season and discussing membrane thickness with your contractor rather than defaulting to the cheapest option.

If you do file an insurance claim for storm-related roof damage, Texas's Prompt Payment Act requires insurers to acknowledge your claim within 15 days and pay or deny it within 60, with 18% annual interest owed on payments that are delayed past that window. You have a two-year window from the date of loss to file.
