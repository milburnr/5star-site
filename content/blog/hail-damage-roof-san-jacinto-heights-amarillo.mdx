If you own a home near San Jacinto Heights and a storm just rolled through, the fastest way to know whether you have hail damage is to check your gutters and metal vents for round dents, then have a licensed roofer confirm what's happening on the shingles themselves. This pocket of Amarillo — the streetcar suburb that grew up along historic Route 66 on Sixth Street — has some of the oldest housing stock in the city, and older roofs take hail worse than newer ones. Potter County sits in the heart of Hail Alley, averaging 8 to 12 hailstorms a year, so knowing what to look for here matters.

## Why Roofs Near Sixth Street Are Especially Vulnerable

The San Jacinto Heights addition west of downtown was platted in the early 20th century, and many of its bungalows and Craftsman homes still carry roofs that have seen decades of Panhandle weather. Two things make these roofs a hail liability.

First, age. A 20- or 25-year-old three-tab shingle has already lost much of its granule armor to UV and wind. When hail hits an aging shingle, it doesn't just bruise — it fractures the brittle mat and knocks loose whatever granules remain. Amarillo sits at 3,600 feet with average winds of 14.3 mph, and that wind drives hail in at an angle, so the west- and south-facing slopes on these older homes absorb the hardest hits.

Second, complexity. The historic homes around Sixth Street tend to have steeper pitches, dormers, and additions built on over the years. Every one of those roofline transitions is a place where flashing can fail and a hailstorm can turn a minor dent into an active leak.

## What to Do Right After a Storm

Speed protects both your home and your claim. Work through these steps in order:

1. **Document before you touch anything.** Photograph gutters, downspouts, window screens, and any visible shingle damage with date stamps.
2. **Cover active leaks.** If water is getting in, tarp the area. The [Texas Department of Insurance](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/tips/storm-damage-tips.html) advises saving receipts for temporary repairs — most policies reimburse them.
3. **Get an independent inspection before the adjuster arrives.** Your own documented scope of damage gives you leverage if the insurer's number comes in low.
4. **File promptly.** You have two years, but on an older roof it's critical to establish that the damage is storm-related and current, not years of accumulated wear.

## Your Rights Under Texas Law

Under the [Texas Prompt Payment Act, Insurance Code Chapter 542](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/GetStatute.aspx?Code=IN&Value=542), your insurer must acknowledge your claim within 15 days, accept or deny it within 60 days, and pay 18% annual interest on any amount delayed past those deadlines. If your carrier drags its feet, cite Chapter 542 in writing — the penalty creates real pressure to move.

Potter County ranks among the highest in the country for hail frequency, with more than 131 hail days recorded since 2000 and a largest-recorded stone of 4.25 inches in May 2019 — softball size. For the older homes near San Jacinto Heights, that history argues strongly for upgrading during any replacement.
