Hail damage on a roof usually doesn't look like the disaster movies suggest — it's granule loss, bruised mats, and cracked seals that are easy to miss from the ground. If you're in Pampa and just came through a hailstorm, the fastest way to know if your roof took a hit is to check for dark, soft-looking dents on the shingles and granules collecting in your gutters and downspouts. Catching it early matters, because minor hail bruising left alone turns into leaks once the shingle mat is exposed to sun and moisture.

## What Hail Damage Actually Looks Like

Hail rarely punches a hole straight through a shingle. More often it leaves a bruise — a soft spot where the impact knocked granules loose and cracked the asphalt mat underneath. From the ground, that can look like a slightly darker or shinier patch on the shingle. Up close, you'll see random, scattered dents rather than the uniform wear pattern of normal aging.

Other signs worth checking: soft metal on gutters, downspouts, and roof vents (hail dents metal before it visibly damages shingles), cracked or missing pieces on ridge caps, and granules pooling at downspout outlets after a storm.

Because most of this damage sits at eye level with a drone or a trained inspector — not from the driveway — a DIY look from the ground will usually miss it. If you're unsure after a storm, that's exactly what a free inspection is for.

## What to Do After a Hailstorm

1. **Document what you can see safely from the ground** — photos of dented gutters, downspout granule buildup, and any visible shingle damage.
2. **Get a professional inspection before filing anything.** An inspector can confirm whether the damage is cosmetic or a covered loss, and give you a written report to attach to a claim.
3. **Notify your insurer promptly.** Under the [Texas Prompt Payment of Claims Act](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/), insurers must acknowledge your claim within 15 days and pay or deny it within 60 days once you've submitted proof of loss — and you generally have up to two years from the date of loss to file. Delayed payments after that window can accrue 18% annual interest.
4. **Keep the inspection report and photos** in case the claim is disputed or you need a second opinion.

## Hail-Damage Considerations Specific to Pampa

Pampa sits in the Texas Panhandle, a stretch of the state that meteorologists sometimes call Hail Alley — the collision of dry desert air moving east and Gulf moisture pushing north produces some of the most hail-active weather in the country. Panhandle communities like Pampa have logged 131 hail days since 2000, with an average of 8 to 12 hailstorms a year, and the region has recorded hail as large as 4.25 inches — softball-sized — in a single event. That frequency is why roof inspections after a storm aren't optional caution here; they're routine maintenance.

If you're planning a roof replacement rather than a repair, it's worth asking your contractor about [Class 4 impact-resistant shingles](https://ibhs.org/wp-content/uploads/member_docs/Relative-Impact-Resistance-of-Asphalt-Shingles_IBHS.pdf), the highest rating under the UL 2218 test standard. A Class 4 shingle has to survive a 2-inch steel ball dropped from 20 feet without breaching — a meaningfully tougher bar than standard shingles, and relevant given how often Panhandle roofs take a hit.

## Repair or Full Replacement?

Not every hail-damaged roof needs to come off. If the damage is limited to a section or two — a few dozen bruised shingles on one slope, for example — a targeted repair can restore the roof's protection without the cost of a full tear-off. A repair usually makes sense when the roof is younger, the damage is contained, and the surrounding shingles are still sealing properly.

Full replacement becomes the better call when hail hit the roof broadly rather than in one spot, when the roof was already close to the end of its expected lifespan, or when an inspector finds damage on multiple layers (shingles, underlayment, and decking). An experienced inspector should be able to tell you which situation you're in and explain why — not just recommend the more expensive option by default. Ask for photos of the specific damage points, not just a verbal summary, so you can see what they saw.
