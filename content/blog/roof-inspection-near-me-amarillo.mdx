A roof inspection near Amarillo means finding a contractor who will physically get on the roof — not just glance at it from the driveway — and check every vulnerable point: shingles, flashing, vents, valleys, and the roof deck underneath. If you're searching for one, you're probably dealing with one of three things: a recent storm, a home sale, or a roof that's getting old enough to worry about.

## What a Real Roof Inspection Covers

A thorough inspection isn't a five-minute look from the ground. It should include:

- **Shingle condition** — granule loss, cracking, curling, or missing tabs
- **Flashing** — around chimneys, vents, skylights, and roof-to-wall transitions, where most leaks actually start
- **Valleys and penetrations** — pipe boots, vent stacks, and any place water concentrates
- **Attic check** — signs of moisture, daylight through the decking, or insulation problems that point to a roof issue from the inside
- **Gutters and drainage** — granule buildup in gutters is often the first visible sign of hail damage

The [Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety](https://ibhs.org/strategy/roof/) treats the roof as the first line of defense on a home and recommends checking it after any severe weather event, not just on a fixed annual schedule — a useful standard for a region that sees severe weather as often as the Panhandle does.

## When to Get One Scheduled

You don't need to wait for a leak. Get an inspection if:

- A hailstorm or high-wind event has passed through in the last few weeks — pair the inspection with a [hail damage assessment](/hail-damage-repair-amarillo/) if you suspect impact damage
- You're buying or selling a home and want documentation of the roof's actual condition
- Your roof is past 15-20 years old and hasn't been checked recently
- You noticed granules collecting in the gutters or downspouts
- An insurance adjuster is coming out and you want your own documentation first

Amarillo sits in an area that's seen 131 hail days since 2000 and typically 8-12 hailstorms a year, with the largest recorded hailstone hitting 4.25 inches back in May 2019. That frequency is exactly why a routine inspection habit matters more here than in most parts of the country — damage accumulates in small increments between the big, obvious storms.

## Amarillo-Specific Considerations

Potter County sits near the center of what's often called Hail Alley, where dry desert air colliding with Gulf moisture produces frequent, sometimes severe hailstorms. That regional pattern means inspections here are less about catching a single catastrophic event and more about tracking cumulative wear — a roof that's taken three or four moderate hail hits over a few years can look fine from the street while carrying real, claimable damage.

If you do end up filing a claim, Texas' Prompt Payment Act requires your insurer to acknowledge the claim within 15 days and pay or deny it within 60, with 18% annual interest owed on payments that are unreasonably delayed. Having a contractor's inspection report and photos in hand before the adjuster visit strengthens your position significantly.
