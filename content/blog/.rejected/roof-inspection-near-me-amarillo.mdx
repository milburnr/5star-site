## When You Actually Need a Roof Inspection

There are three situations that should put a roof inspection on your calendar — not your "someday" list.

**After a hailstorm or major wind event.** Amarillo has recorded 131 hail days, with the largest stone reaching 4.25 inches (softball-sized) in May 2019. Hail damage is not always visible from the ground. Dented pipe boots, cracked ridge caps, and granule loss from shingles are things you'll only catch up close — and [hail damage](/hail-damage-repair-amarillo/) that looks cosmetic from the street can be actively shortening your roof's lifespan. With an average wind speed of 14.3 mph and frequent gusts well above that, wind-lifted shingles are a year-round concern too.

**Before you list the home for sale.** Buyers will order a home inspection. If your roof has hidden damage, it surfaces at the worst possible time — during escrow negotiations. Getting ahead of it with your own inspection gives you the option to repair on your timeline and your terms.

**When the roof is 10-plus years old.** Architectural asphalt shingles in the Texas Panhandle hold up well — plan for 20–25 years with the cooler summers here — but the decade mark is a sensible checkpoint. Flashings around chimneys, skylights, and vents typically show wear before the field shingles do.

## What a Roof Inspector Actually Checks

A thorough inspection is not a two-minute walk around the driveway. A contractor worth hiring will get up on the roof and check:

- **Shingles** — granule loss, cracking, lifting, cupping, or hail strike patterns
- **Flashings** — step flashing at walls, valley metal, chimney counter-flashing, pipe boots
- **Ridge and hip caps** — separation, missing pieces, wind damage
- **Gutters and drip edge** — clogging, rust, improper slope, missing sections
- **Penetrations** — pipe jacks, exhaust caps, skylights — anywhere water can get in
- **Attic ventilation** — ridge vents, turtle vents, soffit vents — because a poorly ventilated attic accelerates shingle aging from the inside out

The Texas Panhandle draws storm-chasers after every large hail event. They knock on doors, offer free inspections, and push homeowners to file claims before the damage is even properly documented. Here's how to tell them apart from a trustworthy local contractor:

**Look for a local address and track record.** A company with a physical Amarillo address and years of work in Potter County has something to protect — their reputation with neighbors and local suppliers. **Ask for a Texas contractor's license.** The [Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/) handles residential roofing contractor registration. Ask to see it.
