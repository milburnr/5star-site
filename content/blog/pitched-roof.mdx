A pitched roof is any roof built with a slope rather than a flat surface — measured as rise over run, so a 6:12 pitch means the roof rises 6 inches vertically for every 12 inches of horizontal run. Almost every house you drive past has a pitched roof of some kind, from a gentle 3:12 ranch-style slope to a steep 12:12 A-frame, and the pitch isn't just an aesthetic choice — it directly affects how well the roof sheds water, what materials can go on it, and how much a replacement costs.

## How Roof Pitch Is Measured and Classified

The [National Roofing Contractors Association](https://www.nrca.net/roofingguidelines/Library/Detail?id=SwYeQcHpLtM%3D) defines a low-slope roof as roughly ¼:12 up to 4:12, and a steep-slope roof as anything greater than 4:12, typically ranging up to about 18:12 on unusually steep designs. That distinction matters practically: low-slope roofs need membrane systems like TPO or modified bitumen that can handle standing water, while steep-slope roofs can use traditional asphalt shingles, metal panels, or tile because gravity does most of the work moving water off.

## Why Pitch Matters for Your Roof

Pitch affects more than looks. A steeper roof:

- **Sheds water and debris faster**, reducing the chance of ponding or granule wear in any one spot
- **Provides more attic space and ventilation options**, which helps with energy efficiency
- **Costs more to reroof**, since crews need more safety equipment and time to work safely at a steeper angle

A lower (but still pitched) roof tends to be more economical to build and maintain, but it needs more attention to flashing and drainage details since water has less slope to work with when it's shedding.

## Pitch Considerations for Texas Panhandle Homes

Most homes in the Amarillo area sit in the 4:12 to 8:12 range — steep enough for good drainage and hail runoff, moderate enough to keep replacement costs reasonable. Given that this region sees 8-12 hailstorms a year, pitch also plays a role in how hail impacts a roof: steeper pitches see hail strike at more of an angle, which can reduce the severity of direct impacts compared to a low-slope surface where hail hits closer to straight-on.
