Hail damage on a roof usually shows up as small dark bruises, cracked or missing granules, and soft spots you can feel more than see — and in Borger, where Hutchinson County sits inside the wider Texas Panhandle hail corridor, most homeowners will deal with it more than once over the life of a roof. The tricky part isn't spotting a hole in the shingle. It's recognizing damage subtle enough to pass a glance from the ground but serious enough to shorten your roof's life by years if it's ignored.

## What Hail Damage Actually Looks Like

On asphalt shingles, hail impacts leave circular bruises where the granules have been knocked loose, exposing the black asphalt mat underneath. Fresh damage often looks shiny or slightly darker than the surrounding shingle. Given enough time and sun exposure, those bruised spots start to crack, and water works its way in at the weak point.

Metal roofing shows hail differently — dents rather than granule loss — and while dents are mostly cosmetic on standing seam panels, large enough hail can dent through protective coatings and expose bare metal to rust. Gutters, vents, and window screens usually show impact marks too, and they're a good secondary indicator even if you can't get a clear look at the roof itself.

## How to Check Your Roof After a Storm

Start at ground level with binoculars before you consider getting on a ladder. Look for:

- Dented or dinged gutters, downspouts, and roof vents
- Granules collecting in gutters or at the base of downspouts
- Dark spots or bruising visible on shingles from an upstairs window
- Cracked or chipped ridge caps

If you see any of these signs, the next step is a professional inspection rather than a DIY climb — hail-softened shingles are easy to damage further just by walking on them, and a trained eye can tell the difference between cosmetic wear and damage an insurance adjuster will recognize.

## Borger-Specific Considerations

[Class 4 impact-resistant shingles](https://www.ibhs.org/roofing/impact-resistant-roofing-cover-boards/) — the highest UL 2218 rating — hold up significantly better against the large hail common to this part of the Panhandle, and some Texas insurers offer premium discounts for installing them. That's worth asking about here specifically, since Hutchinson County sits inside the same hail corridor that gives the wider Amarillo area 8-12 hailstorms a year, with recorded hail up to 4.25 inches in diameter as recently as May 2019.

Texas law also works in your favor once you file: the [Texas Prompt Payment Act](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/) requires insurers to acknowledge your claim within 15 days and pay or deny it within 60, with 18% annual interest accruing on payments that run late. You have a 2-year window from the date of loss to file, so there's no need to rush a claim before you've had a proper inspection — but there's also no reason to wait once damage is documented.
