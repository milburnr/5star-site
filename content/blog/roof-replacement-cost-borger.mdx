A roof replacement in Borger typically runs **$15,000 to $18,000** for a standard single-story home with architectural shingles, based on Xactimate insurance-grade pricing across the Texas Panhandle as of April 2026. That range covers a straightforward 20-square gable roof; steep pitches, second stories, and cut-up rooflines push costs higher. Borger sits in the same regional pricing band as Amarillo, so there's no metro-market premium to account for the way there is in Texas's larger metro areas.

## What Actually Drives the Price

Roofing is priced per "square" — 100 square feet of roof surface. A **20 SQ** roof corresponds to roughly a 1,450 sqft single-story footprint at a 6:12 pitch, which works out to about 2,000 sqft of actual roof surface once pitch and waste are factored in. On a simple single-story gable at that size, the typical breakdown looks like this:

- Tear-off, haul, and disposal: **$56.62/SQ**
- Synthetic underlayment: **$47.89/SQ**
- Laminated architectural shingle, installed: **$284.01/SQ**
- Valley metal (standard in Texas scopes): **$7.33/LF**
- Ridge vent and hip/ridge cap: **$11.69/LF** and **$6.28/LF**
- Pipe jacks, exhaust caps, and flashing: itemized per penetration

That adds up to a roof subtotal of roughly **$12,180** on a simple gable, and a grand total near **$15,035** once overhead, profit, and sales tax are included. On a per-square-foot basis, that's about **$7.00 to $7.75/sqft** all-in for a simple single-story roof — figures pulled directly from Xactimate estimates, not retail calculator averages, which tend to run 15-25% low for insurance-grade work.

## Simple Roof vs. Complex Roof Pricing

Not every roof is a simple gable. A 2-story home with a 7-9/12 pitch and a partial hip roof — still 20 squares — moves the numbers up meaningfully. A real Xactimate export for that scope came in at a **$17,820 grand total**, with a roof subtotal of $14,488 and an effective **$8.91/sqft** all-in. That's about an 18-19% premium over the simple-gable baseline, driven by the high-roof charge, steep-pitch labor factor, and the extra flashing footage a hip roofline requires.

Other modifiers that stack onto either scenario:

- **Very steep pitch (10-12/12):** +18-25% of the roof subtotal
- **Each chimney, skylight, or large penetration:** +$250-$700
- **Class 4 impact-resistant shingle upgrade:** +$85-$125/SQ
- **Decking replacement**, if the tear-off reveals rotten OSB or plywood: +$2.25-$3.50/sqft of affected area

One line item that's *not* standard in Texas: ice-and-water barrier. Most Panhandle jurisdictions don't require it by code, so it's typically excluded from the baseline scope — unlike colder climates where it's a default line item.

## Borger-Specific Considerations

Borger sits well inside the Panhandle's hail alley, the same regional weather pattern that makes Potter County — just south in Amarillo — rank in the nation's top 10 for hail frequency. The region sees an average of 8-12 hailstorms a year, and the Panhandle has logged 131 hail days since 2000, including a softball-sized 4.25-inch hailstone recorded in May 2019. If your roof damage is storm-related, that context matters for your insurance claim, not just your repair estimate.

Texas law works in your favor on timing: under the [Texas Insurance Code's prompt payment provisions](https://law.justia.com/codes/texas/insurance-code/title-5/subtitle-c/chapter-542/subchapter-b/section-542-056/), your insurer must acknowledge a claim within 15 days and accept or deny it within 60 days of receiving your proof of loss. Insurers that miss those deadlines owe 18% annual interest on the delayed payment, and you have a 2-year window from the date of loss to file. A written, itemized estimate — the kind an Xactimate scope produces — gives your adjuster less room to lowball the claim.

## What to Ask For in a Quote

Not every estimate breaks costs down the same way, and that makes comparing bids harder than it should be. A retail cash bid from a contractor and an insurance-grade Xactimate scope aren't the same document — retail bids often bundle materials and skip itemized upgrades, while an insurance scope prices out every line: tear-off, underlayment, starter course, valley metal, ventilation, and flashing separately, with overhead and profit applied on qualifying claims. When you're comparing quotes for a Borger roof replacement, ask for:

- A **per-square breakdown**, not a single lump-sum number
- Confirmation of **1-layer vs. 2-layer tear-off** — a second layer adds $30-$50/SQ over a single-layer job
- Whether **valley metal** is included (it should be — open valleys with W-valley metal are standard practice in Texas)
- Whether the bid assumes **existing decking is sound**, or includes a contingency line for rot found after tear-off
- The manufacturer and **wind/impact rating** of the shingle being quoted, especially if you're considering the Class 4 upgrade for hail resistance

A written, itemized quote also gives you a paper trail if you end up filing an insurance claim later — adjusters take a detailed scope more seriously than a one-line estimate.
