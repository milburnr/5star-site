A standing seam metal roof in Borger runs **$13.00 to $18.00 per square foot installed** as of April 2026, based on current Xactimate pricing for the Texas Panhandle market. For a typical 2,000-square-foot roof surface, that puts the job in the **$26,000 to $36,000 range** — roughly double what the same house would cost with architectural asphalt shingles.

That gap is the first question every Borger homeowner asks once they start pricing metal. Here's what's actually driving it, and where the cost trade-off starts to make sense.

## Why standing seam costs almost double what shingles do

Architectural shingle replacement in the Panhandle runs about **$7.00 to $7.75 per square foot** for a simple one-story gable roof, with a typical 20-square (2,000 sqft roof surface) job landing around **$15,035 all-in** — that figure comes from a real Xactimate estimate for a 20 SQ simple gable roof in Amarillo, priced April 2026. Standing seam metal starts at nearly double that per-square-foot rate.

The premium comes from the panels themselves (roll-formed or factory-fabricated steel or aluminum with a baked-on finish), the concealed fastener system that hides screws under interlocking seams instead of exposing them to weather, and the specialized crew labor — metal roofing isn't a trade every shingle crew is trained to install correctly. Get the seam spacing or fastener placement wrong and you're looking at leaks within a few years, which is why pricing runs a real premium over commodity asphalt.

That premium buys a lot of longevity, though: properly installed standing seam panels [carry a 40-60 year life expectancy](https://blog.mcelroymetal.com/metal-roofing-and-siding/what-is-the-life-expectancy-of-a-metal-roof) from manufacturers like McElroy Metal, roughly double what architectural shingles hold up for in this climate.

## What pushes a Borger quote toward $18/sqft instead of $13

A handful of factors stack on top of the baseline price:

- **Roof complexity.** A simple gable roof prices at the low end of the range. Add a second story, steeper pitch (7-12/12 or steeper), hips, valleys, or multiple dormers, and labor time climbs fast — metal panels require precise cuts and custom flashing at every transition.
- **Deck condition.** If the tear-off reveals rotted or damaged decking, replacing OSB or plywood adds to the job before a single panel goes down.
- **Penetrations.** Every chimney, skylight, and vent stack needs custom flashing work on a metal roof, which takes more time than shingle flashing.
- **Panel gauge and finish.** Heavier-gauge steel and premium Kynar finishes cost more than lighter-gauge, standard-finish options.

Since Borger sits in the same Panhandle pricing tier as Amarillo, none of the metro-market premiums that apply farther south and east in Texas factor in here — the $13-$18/sqft range reflects Panhandle labor and material costs directly, not a discount or markup for market.

## Borger-Specific Considerations

Hutchinson County sits inside the same hail corridor that gives the Panhandle its reputation — the broader region has logged **131 hail days since 2000**, with **8 to 12 hailstorms a year** on average. That frequency is exactly why metal roofing keeps coming up in conversations with Borger homeowners: a properly installed standing seam roof shrugs off hail that would crack or granule-strip an asphalt shingle roof.

If you're weighing a metal roof after storm damage, know your timeline rights under Texas law. The [Texas Prompt Payment Act](https://www.tdi.texas.gov/) requires insurers to acknowledge your claim within 15 days and pay or deny it within 60 — and if they're late, they owe 18% annual interest on the delayed amount. That's worth knowing before you accept a lowball settlement or let an adjuster string out the process.

## Getting an accurate number for your roof

Every figure above is a range because your actual quote depends on your roof's exact pitch, square footage, complexity, and deck condition — the only way to get a firm number is an on-site inspection. That's true whether you're comparing metal against shingles or just trying to budget for a replacement.

A written quote should break out tear-off, decking repair (if any), panel material and gauge, flashing and trim work, and labor as separate line items — the same way an insurance-grade Xactimate estimate does. If a contractor hands you a single lump-sum number with no breakdown, ask for the itemized version before you sign anything. It's the only way to know whether you're comparing apples to apples against other bids.
